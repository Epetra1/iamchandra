01 / The research, in brief.

The research, in brief.

Network configuration is a fundamental part of network management, but translating operational requirements into correct device-level configurations remains difficult because configurations are precise, vendor-specific and sensitive to small errors. Large Language Models (LLMs) provide a promising way to translate natural-language intents into configurations, yet existing studies use different models, datasets, vendors and evaluation methods, making it difficult to determine which techniques actually improve accuracy and whether their benefits justify the computational cost.

This research investigates how an open-weight LLM-based pipeline can generate syntactically and logically accurate Cisco IOS and Juniper Junos configurations while remaining computationally efficient. The study uses a controlled experimental design to evaluate fine-tuning and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), individually and in combination, across selected Qwen and Llama models. Selected models will then be evaluated within an agentic workflow that uses external validation and bounded iterative correction. Evaluation will combine reference-based measures with syntactic and logical accuracy, latency and computational-cost measurements, using a separate multi-vendor benchmark for consistent comparison.

The initial findings show that the required dataset, fine-tuning process and evaluation environment are available to carry out the proposed research. They also show that broader training can affect Qwen and Llama differently, supporting the need for controlled comparison. These findings demonstrate the feasibility of the research rather than provide final answers to the research questions. The remaining experiments will identify which techniques improve accuracy and efficiency and will use this evidence to design the final network-configuration pipeline.

03 / What this research asks.

What this research asks.

Main research question

How can an open-weight LLM-based pipeline be designed to translate natural-language network intents into syntactically and logically accurate Cisco IOS and Juniper Junos configurations while remaining computationally efficient?

Fine-tuning + retrieval

How do domain-specific fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation, individually and in combination, affect syntactic accuracy, logical accuracy and computational cost?

Agentic validation

To what extent does tool-assisted validation and iterative correction improve accuracy, and what additional computational cost does it introduce?

What the results suggest

Qwen gained 44 new exact matches with no regressions. Llama gained three and lost two; its Line F1 declined. Zero-shot Exact Match remained 0% for both models.

Dataset composition, formatting and sequence length changed between runs. These preliminary results cannot isolate a single cause. The next stage uses controlled comparisons and operational validation.
